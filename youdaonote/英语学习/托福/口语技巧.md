The passage talks about audience effects which means that individuals' work is affected by their knowledge that they are visible to others. When we know we're being watched as we perform some activity, we tend to increase the speed of doing it. The professor used two examples to demonstrate this. In the example of tying shoes, the group of students with the knowledge of being observed tied their shoes much faster than the other group. In the example of learning to type, people who are conscious of being observed typed a lot faster than they would if they were alone, but they also tend to make more mistakes at the same time, that is another common behavior.

The two examples basically explained the meaning of audience effects. （最后一句是总结句，考生可以根据自己的回答时间选择性舍弃。）

In this lecture, the professor talks about two ways that learning art skills can benefit a young child's development. One way is that drawing provides young children with limited vocabulary a platform to express complex emotions, for example, a little girl can communicate the feeling of pride by drawing herself jumping up in the air next to her bike. In the drawing, her arms are raised up in the air and she's smiling. Children can draw to communicate their emotions better than they could with words. Another way is that art skills can help children to develop patience and concentration to persist in an activity, suppose there's a little boy who wants to mold a lump of clay into the shape of a car, It’s likely that he is dissatisfied with his fist attempt, and then he would try again and again to improve his work to his satisfaction. The newly shaped clay car is an instant reminder of success resulting from his persistence. The boy may be able to transfer this lesson toward other situations and activities because he's had the experience of successfully accomplishing a goal through persistence.

So these are the two ways presented by the professor that learning art can help a child's development.
