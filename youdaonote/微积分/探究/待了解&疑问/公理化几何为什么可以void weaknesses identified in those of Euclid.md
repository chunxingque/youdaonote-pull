The text Grundlagen der Geometrie (tr.: Foundations of Geometry) published by Hilbert in 1899 proposes a formal set, called Hilbert's axioms, substituting for the traditional axioms of Euclid. They avoid weaknesses identified in those of Euclid, whose works at the time were still used textbook-fashion.

Hilbert's approach signaled the shift to the modern axiomatic method. 



Though he had

managed to apply the axiomatic method with success in showing the 

completeness and consistency of geometry, Hubert had only been able to do so in relative

terms, assuming the consistency of the real numbers.
