Dense as the rational numbers are,

they do not suffice as a theoretical basis of measurement by numbers.



Because the system of rational numbers is not sufficient for geometry, it is necessary to invent new numbers as measures of incommensurable quantities: these new numbers are called "irrational."

The general idea of the irrational number appeared first at the end

of the sixteenth century as a consequence of the introduction of decimal

fractions, the use of which became established at that time in connection

with the appearance of logarithmic tables. 



It was not until the sixth decade of the nineteenth century that the

need was felt for a more precise arithmetic formulation of the 

foundations of irrational numbers. This occurred in the lectures which

Weierstrass delivered at about that date.



concept of irrational number belongs certainly only to mathematics of

precision.
