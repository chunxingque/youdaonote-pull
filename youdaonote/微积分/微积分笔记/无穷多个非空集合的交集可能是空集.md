For every positive real number $$\epsilon > 0$$, the interval $$(0,\epsilon)$$
is non-empty because of $$0 < \epsilon$$，but the intersection of all nonempty
intervals $$\bigcap_{\varepsilon > 0}^{}{(0,\epsilon)} = \varnothing$$.

Proof I. If $$\bigcap_{\varepsilon > 0}^{}{(0,\epsilon)}$$ is not empty, then
there must be a number $$\delta$$ in $$\bigcap_{\varepsilon >
0}^{}{(0,\epsilon)}$$, obviously $$\delta$$ should be a positive real number but
it also has to be smaller than every positive real number, which lead to the
contradiction that $$\delta < \delta$$.

Proof II. For every positive real number $$\epsilon > 0$$, $$\epsilon \notin
\left( 0,\epsilon \right)$$, hence $$\epsilon \notin \bigcap_{\varepsilon >
0}^{}{(0,\epsilon)}$$. (The proof is inspired by Examples 2.10 in Walter Rodin’s
Principles of Mathematical Analysis (1976))
