The pioneers of calculus  knew that their approach in using infinitesimals rested on precarious logical foundations, but for the most part they didn’t care. As long as their method led to correct results, they reasoned, it must be fundamentally sound. Others, however, were not so sanguine. Critics from Bishop George Berkeley charged that infinitesimals undermine mathematics and even rationality itself, and would inevitably lead to serious errors. And so the debate over infinitesimals raged.
