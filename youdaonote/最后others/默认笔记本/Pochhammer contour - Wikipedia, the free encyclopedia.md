The exact position, curvature, etc. are in this case not essential; the sequence of windings around the two special points is.In mathematics, the **Pochhammer contour**, introduced by [Camille Jordan](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Jordan "Camille Jordan") (1887) and [Leo Pochhammer](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Pochhammer "Leo Pochhammer") (1890), is a contour in the [complex plane](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_plane "Complex plane") with two points removed, used for [contour integration](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contour_integration "Contour integration"). If *A* and *B* are loops around the two points, both starting at some fixed point *P*, then the Pochhammer contour is the commutator *ABA*−1*B*−1, where the superscript −1 denotes a path taken in the opposite direction. With the two points taken as 0 and 1, the fixed basepoint *P* being on the real axis between them, an example is the path that starts at *P*, encircles the point 1 in the counter-clockwise direction and returns to *P*, then encircles 0 counter-clockwise and returns to *P*, after that circling 1 and then 0 clockwise, before coming back to *P*. The class of the contour is an actual [commutator](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commutator "Commutator") when it is considered in the [fundamental group](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_group "Fundamental group") with basepoint *P* of the complement in the complex plane (or [Riemann sphere](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riemann_sphere "Riemann sphere")) of the two points looped. When it comes to taking contour integrals, moving basepoint from *P* to another choice *Q* makes no difference to the result, since there will be cancellation of integrals from *P* to *Q* and back.

[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pochhammer_contour&action=edit§ion=1 "Edit section: Homologous to zero but not homotopic to zero")]Homologous to zero but not homotopic to zero
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Within the doubly punctured plane this curve is [homologous](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homology_group "Homology group") to zero but not [homotopic](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homotopy_group "Homotopy group") to zero. Its [winding number](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winding_number "Winding number") about any point is 0 despite the fact that within the doubly punctured plane it cannot be shrunk to a single point.

[![](attachments/Pochhammer%20contour%20-%20Wikipedia,%20the%20free%20encyclopedia_image_1.png)](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pochhammer.homology2.svg)[![](http://en.wikipedia.org//bits.wikimedia.org/static-1.20wmf8/skins/common/images/magnify-clip.png)](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Pochhammer.homology2.svg "Enlarge")Pochhammer cycle is homologous to zero: it is the boundary of the green area minus the boundary of the red one.[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pochhammer_contour&action=edit§ion=2 "Edit section: Applications")]Applications
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The [beta function](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_function "Beta function") is given by [Euler's](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonhard_Euler "Leonhard Euler") integral

![\displaystyle \Beta(\alpha,\beta)=\int_0^1 t^{\alpha-1}(1-t)^{\beta-1} \, dt ](attachments/Pochhammer%20contour%20-%20Wikipedia,%20the%20free%20encyclopedia_image_2.png)provided that the real parts of *α* and *β* are positive, which may be converted into an integral over the Pochhammer contour *C* as

![\displaystyle (1-e^{2\pi i\alpha})(1-e^{2\pi i\beta})\Beta(\alpha,\beta) =\int_C t^{\alpha-1}(1-t)^{\beta-1} \, dt.](attachments/Pochhammer%20contour%20-%20Wikipedia,%20the%20free%20encyclopedia_image_3.png)The contour integral converges for all values of *α* and *β* and so gives the [analytic continuation](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analytic_continuation "Analytic continuation") of the beta function. A similar method can be applied to Euler's integral for the [hypergeometric function](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergeometric_function "Hypergeometric function") to give its analytic continuation.

[[edit](http://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pochhammer_contour&action=edit§ion=3 "Edit section: Puzzle")]Puzzle
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A popular puzzle asks how one can hang a picture on a wall by looping a string over two nails in such a way that if either one of the nails is removed, the picture falls. The Pochhammer contour is one answer. The picture may be attached at any point on the curve.
