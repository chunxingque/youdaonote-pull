What is Coursera?

Coursera is a social entrepreneurship company that partners with top universities around the world to offer free online courses. Founded by two Stanford computer science professors, Coursera envisions a future where the top universities are able to educate anyone who has access to the Internet. Coursera’s online platform enables top professors to teach hundreds of thousands of students at one time.

(Source: <https://www.coursera.org/about>)

Resource type: Open traditional university courses (Knowledge)

Time Commitment: 4-12 weeks/course

Level of expertise: From freshmen to seasoned scholars

Subject areas: Computer science, humanities and social sciences, mathematics and statistics, healthcare and medicine, economics and finance, society and networks.

(Looking for online courses in a different subject? Try the [OpenCourseWare Consortium](http://www.ocwconsortium.org/en/courses/catalog).)

Tutorial

*Purpose of this tutorial*

The purpose is to show you how to navigate through the Coursera course marketplace and walk through a course from registration to completion. We will also offer some suggestions for how to maximize your course experience.

 

*Signing up for classes*

When you first register and login to Coursera, click on the Courses link in the top menu. You will come to a page that lists all the classes available to sign up for. There plenty of choices so pick which ones interest you the most!

[![](attachments/The%20UnCollege%20Guide%20to%20Coursera%20%20UnCollege_image_1.png)](http://www.uncollege.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/Coursera1.png)

 

To sign up for a course, click on the course title. After you land on the course home page, click the blue Sign Up button on the center-left side of the page to formally enroll:

[![](attachments/The%20UnCollege%20Guide%20to%20Coursera%20%20UnCollege_image_2.png)](http://www.uncollege.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/Coursera-Sign-Up.png)

*Get started learning* 

After you have signed up for a course, the Sign Up button on the course home page is replaced by the following:

[![](attachments/The%20UnCollege%20Guide%20to%20Coursera%20%20UnCollege_image_3.png)](http://www.uncollege.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/Coursera-Go-To-Class.png)

Click on Go to Class! to enter the course portal. On the left side of the course portal is the main course navigation menu. On the right side are upcoming deadlines and events.

[![](attachments/The%20UnCollege%20Guide%20to%20Coursera%20%20UnCollege_image_4.png)](http://www.uncollege.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/Coursera-Syllabus.png)

This course website contains all of the course’s information and makes it easier for professors and students to communicate with each other. Also, not all classes have the same menu items, but most of them have the following:

* Syllabus
* Homework assignments
* Quizzes
* Video lectures
* Discussion forum

 

*Video lectures*

Click on Video Lectures to access all of the class lectures and slides.

 

*Homework*

Click on Homeworks and you will see all the assignments, solutions (with explanations), and their due dates.

[![](attachments/The%20UnCollege%20Guide%20to%20Coursera%20%20UnCollege_image_5.png)](http://www.uncollege.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/Coursera-Quiz1.png)

*Quizzes*

Click on Quizzes, and you will see all of the quizzes for the course. Coursera allows you to take these quizzes online.

[![](attachments/The%20UnCollege%20Guide%20to%20Coursera%20%20UnCollege_image_6.png)](http://www.uncollege.org/wp-content/uploads/2012/05/Coursera-Quiz-2.png)

Tips and tricks for a great Coursera experience

* *Create your own personal learning plan*Coursera is just one of many online learning resources. Set clear goals and guidelines for what you want to accomplish and integrate Coursera into other parts of your self-directed learning curriculum so you are getting the most out of the experience.See [DIY U’s Personal Learning Plan](http://p2pu.org/en/groups/diy-u/) for examples, templates, and other great resources on this topic.
* *Take just one course vs. as many as you can*With so many courses available, it might be tempting to take as many courses offered as possible, but that would really hurt your focus and potentially your course outcomes. So start with one, and take more classes if you are comfortable. Don’t overextend yourself when you’re just getting started—you want to make sure you’ll stick with it!
* *Build a side-project after you finish each course*After you finish a course, don’t just assume your knowledge acquisition is done. For example, for a programming course, start working on some side-projects that utilize the skills you gained in the course like:

* A calculator
* A personal website
* A professional portfolio
* A blog
* A to-do list
* A search engine
* A photo-sharing app
* A calendar app
* A forum app
